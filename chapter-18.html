Chapter 18 of 20

Safety, Interactions, and Questions to Ask

A multi-ingredient supplement deserves a full safety review. An ingredient that is suitable for one person may be unsuitable for someone else because of health conditions, age, pregnancy status, kidney or liver function, or prescription medicines. Combining ingredients also makes it harder to predict the overall effect.

CoQ10 may interact with warfarin and insulin, according to the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health. Magnesium can reduce absorption of certain antibiotics and oral bisphosphonates when taken too close together. Curcumin products designed for increased absorption have been associated with reports of liver injury. These examples are not a complete interaction list.

People with diabetes, kidney or liver disease, seizure disorders, bleeding concerns, or complex medication regimens should ask a pharmacist or clinician to review the exact Supplement Facts label before using the product. The same applies to anyone preparing for surgery, pregnant, or breastfeeding. Do not stop or alter prescribed medicines to accommodate a supplement.

Stop using a supplement and seek medical advice if you develop a concerning reaction. Serious symptoms such as chest pain, fainting, difficulty breathing, severe allergic symptoms, yellowing of the skin or eyes, or dark urine require prompt medical attention. Keep the bottle and label available so the ingredients and lot information can be identified.
