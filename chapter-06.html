Coenzyme Q10 and the Energy-Transfer Chain

Coenzyme Q10, commonly called CoQ10, is a compound that the body can make and that is present in many tissues. It plays a role in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, where electrons are passed between protein complexes as part of the process that helps establish the gradient used to make ATP. CoQ10 also functions as an antioxidant in cell membranes.

Because CoQ10 has a recognizable role in mitochondrial energy production, it appears in many products marketed for cellular energy. Advanced Mitochondrial Formula lists CoQ10 as one component of its blend. However, the fact that the body uses CoQ10 does not mean that extra CoQ10 will necessarily raise energy levels or improve health for everyone.

Research on CoQ10 has examined different conditions and outcomes, with results varying by population and disease. Evidence for one medical condition cannot be transferred automatically to general fatigue or to a multi-ingredient product. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health notes that evidence is limited for many proposed uses and identifies potential interactions.

CoQ10 may interact with warfarin and insulin, and it may be incompatible with some cancer treatments. Anyone taking these medicines or undergoing cancer treatment should discuss CoQ10-containing supplements with a clinician before use. The full ingredient list matters because this product combines CoQ10 with several other compounds.
