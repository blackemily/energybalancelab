Niacinamide and the NAD Pathway

Niacinamide is one form of vitamin B3. It is also known as nicotinamide. The body uses vitamin B3 to build coenzymes including NAD and NADP, which take part in many reactions involved in energy metabolism and other essential cell functions. NAD exists in different chemical states and helps transfer electrons during metabolic reactions.

The manufacturer identifies niacinamide as one of the ingredients in Advanced Mitochondrial Formula and discusses its relationship to NAD and ATP production. This is a legitimate biochemical connection: NAD-related molecules are important to cellular metabolism. But it is important not to turn that connection into a promise that a particular supplement will restore youthful energy or reverse an age-related process.

Niacinamide is not exactly the same as nicotinic acid, another form of vitamin B3 often simply called niacin. The two forms can have different effects and side-effect profiles. High-dose use of vitamin B3 products is not automatically harmless, and the right intake depends on the form, amount, health circumstances, and other products a person uses.

When reviewing this ingredient, look at the actual amount on the current Supplement Facts label rather than relying on a marketing description. People taking multiple supplements, managing diabetes, liver disease, or other chronic conditions, or using prescription medicines should discuss the full product with a healthcare professional. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements provides an evidence-based overview of niacin and its forms.
